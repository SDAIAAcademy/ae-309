The brief

A service for Al-Manar that meets a budget you stated

Inspection

Find potholes and patches, or segment cracks, in road frames. Accuracy is average precision or mask overlap, by lighting and district.

Counting

Count vehicles across a line in traffic clips. Accuracy is count error per clip, with identity switches reported beside it.

You wrote the budget in lab 4.4: a per-frame latency at the 95th percentile on the roadside unit, an accuracy floor, and at least three field conditions.

Either task may start from the course starter (the supplied checkpoints and roadkit) or from the participant's own model, and may use the course's synthetic pack or a public road-damage set under its own licence. What is assessed is the evidence, not the size of the model. AI assistants are unrestricted in the capstone; the defence shows who understands the result.

The repository

Six things the assessor looks for in the public repository

Data card and split report

What the data is, the guideline, the agreement score, no site shared across splits.

Field-conditions test set

Paired with the clean test set, and how it was made.

Benchmark table

Latency, size and accuracy per operating point, the machine named, the chosen row marked.

Model card

Results per condition and district with intervals, the privacy controls and their measured recall, the monitors.

Release memo

One page: release, release with a human check, or hold, per condition, against a rule stated in advance.

The trail

Training logs, checkpoints, commit history, and one command that re-runs the evaluation.

The trail is the authenticity evidence: logs, checkpoints and a commit history that shows the work growing. In the defence the participant re-runs the evaluation on a held-back condition and explains the result, so the one command must work on the day. Faces and plates must not appear readable anywhere in a repository; the synthetic street frames are drawings.

How the repository is scored

The rubric

25%
Latency budget
20%
Field-condition tests and privacy
25%
Reproducibility
10%
A claim without a number is a plan, not a result. And a number without its condition, its count and its interval is half a result.

Walk through each criterion with what full marks look like. Data and splits: a split report with no shared site and an agreement score for any label the participant added. Model quality and error analysis: the metric, an error breakdown by type and a justified next step. Latency budget: a table measured at more than one operating point, accuracy re-measured after each change. Field-condition tests and privacy: drops per condition with intervals, a blur recall, a decision. Reproducibility: the evaluation re-runs from a clean clone. Overall weighting of the course: the repository 60%, the live defence 20%, the supervised entry and exit checks 20%; passing is 70% overall and at least 60% on this rubric.

Format

Eight minutes of talk, then four minutes on a failure the assessor picks

1 · The budget and the result

The task, the budget you stated, the operating point you chose. Two minutes.

2 · The evidence

The table by condition with intervals, the error breakdown, the release decision. Four minutes.

3 · What you would not release

The weakest condition, what happens under it, the next step. Two minutes.

Then the failure case. The assessor shows one frame or clip your service gets wrong, and names a held-back condition. You re-run the evaluation on it and explain what you see.

The catalog entry asks for a defence on a failure case the assessor picks and states no lengths: eight and four minutes are this edition's choice. Pick the failure case from the participant's own error analysis or from a condition they did not test. Good questions: which error type is this, what would fix it and what would the fix cost, would your monitors have noticed, does your release memo cover it. After each defence everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.
